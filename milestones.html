Milestones

A milestone marks a significant point in a project: a moment when something important is finished or approved. Milestones don't take time themselves; they tell everyone where the project stands.

PMBOK definition: A milestone is a significant point or event in a project. It has zero duration and uses no resources. Milestones are listed in the milestone list, an output of Define Activities.

Milestone vs. activity vs. deliverable

People mix these up constantly. The difference is what each one represents.

◆

Milestone

A point in time. Zero duration, done or not done.

"Design approved"

▬

Activity

Work that takes time and resources. Has a start, a finish, and a duration.

"Design the user interface" (4 days)

▣

Deliverable

A product or result produced by activities. Approving it is often a milestone.

"UI design document"

Milestone or activity?

Sort each item. A good test: if you can say "we're halfway through it", it's an activity. A milestone is either reached or not.

Types of milestones

  • Mandatory: required by a contract, law, or the sponsor. E.g. "Regulatory approval received", "Payment milestone 2".
  • Optional: set by the team to track progress. E.g. "Prototype demoed to teachers".
  • Phase gates: a milestone at the end of a phase where the sponsor decides to continue, change, or stop.

What makes a good milestone

  • Binary: clearly reached or not. "Design 80% done" is not a milestone.
  • Verifiable: tied to an approval or sign-off someone can show.
  • Meaningful: marks a real step forward stakeholders care about.
  • Evenly spaced: roughly every few weeks, so problems show up early.
  • Named as an outcome: "Requirements signed off", not "Do requirements".

📝 PMP exam tips

  • Milestones have zero duration. If an option gives a milestone a duration, it's wrong.
  • The milestone list is an output of Define Activities; it states whether each milestone is mandatory or optional.
  • A milestone chart is a summary schedule for senior stakeholders. Detailed bar charts are for the team.
  • A milestone schedule can appear in the project charter as a summary of key dates.
  • Contracts often tie payments to milestones. Missing one can have financial consequences.
  • In agile, sprint reviews and release dates serve as milestones.
