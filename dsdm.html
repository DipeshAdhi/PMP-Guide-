Agile · DSDM

DSDM: agile with firm foundations

The Dynamic Systems Development Method is an agile project framework. It adds what Scrum leaves out: a feasibility check, just enough up-front foundations, and fixed delivery dates. Time and cost are fixed; the features flex, using MoSCoW priorities.

Turn the triangle upside down

DSDM's core idea. Instead of fixing the features and letting the date and budget slip, fix the date, budget, and quality, then deliver the most important features within them.

Traditional

Scope is agreed up front. When it grows, the deadline and budget absorb it, often with quality cut at the end.

DSDM

The date never moves. If time runs short, lower-priority features (Coulds) are dropped, never quality.

The DSDM lifecycle

Click any phase. Feasibility and Foundations are done once (and revisited if needed); Evolutionary Development and Deployment repeat for each increment.

MoSCoW: what fits in the increment?

The first increment has 40 days of team effort and a fixed delivery date. Requirements add up to more than that. Give every requirement a priority. DSDM's rule of thumb: Musts no more than 60% of the effort, with around 20% Coulds as contingency.

Inside Evolutionary Development: exploration, engineering, timeboxes

The solution evolves in short, fixed timeboxes (typically 2–4 weeks). Each timebox can focus on exploration (what the business needs) or engineering (making it robust), or both.

🔭 Exploration

Work with the Business Ambassador to explore and demonstrate functional needs: workshops, prototypes, models. Answers "is this what you need?"

🛠️ Engineering

Turn what was explored into a solution that's ready for real use: performance, security, maintainability, testing. Answers "will it work in production?"

📝 Exam tips (PMP & PMI-ACP)

  • DSDM fixes time, cost, and quality; features are the variable.
  • MoSCoW comes from DSDM: Must, Should, Could, Won't (this time). Musts ≤ 60% of effort.
  • Feasibility → Foundations → Evolutionary Development → Deployment, framed by Pre-Project and Post-Project.
  • Foundations is "enough design up front", not a full specification.
  • The Business Ambassador is the business voice inside the team, roughly like a Product Owner.
  • Timeboxing is non-negotiable: the date is kept by dropping Coulds, then Shoulds, never quality.
